# Panel sensitivity

15 briefs (5 headliners x 3 cities), three disjoint comparison panels.

| panel | acts | same first room as default | top 3 shared with default (avg of 3) | first room differs from plain affinity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| default | Taylor Swift, Metallica, Kendrick Lamar, Luke Combs, Billie Eilish | 15/15 | 3.0 | 13/15 |
| B | Drake, Coldplay, Morgan Wallen, Bad Bunny, Foo Fighters | 11/15 | 2.5 | 10/15 |
| C | Beyoncé, Ed Sheeran, Zach Bryan, Travis Scott, Dua Lipa | 12/15 | 2.5 | 12/15 |

## Reading it

- The panel matters, moderately. With a different panel the first room is the same in 11 to 12 of 15 briefs, and about 2.5 of the top 3 rooms are shared. Roughly one brief in four changes its first room, usually to another room already in the same eight.
- The reordering matters more than the panel: the first room differs from plain affinity order in 10 to 13 of 15 briefs under every panel.
- The reorder never promotes a room Qloo did not already put in the headliner's top eight by affinity, so a room the audience only mildly likes cannot jump in from outside. Inside those eight it can move up on a large "vs other acts" lift. Both numbers are shown on every line: Qloo's affinity, and the lift beside it.
- What this does not show: that the first room is the better booking. That needs capacity and sales history. This only shows how much of the order is the panel's doing, and the answer is: some, so treat the order as a prompt to look at the room, not a verdict.
